## 1. What the market charges

Researched August 2026.

| Product | Monthly | Commission | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Fresha** | £0 (free tier) | **20% on new marketplace clients** | "Free" software; the marketplace commission is the business model |
| **Treatwell** | £50–150 | **5–8% of bookings** | Commission-led; strong consumer marketplace |
| **Booksy Biz** | ~£24 + ~£16/extra staff | None | ~£55/mo for 3 chairs, ~£88 for 5 |
| **Squire** | ~£24 + ~£16/extra staff | None | Barber-specific, US-centric |
| **Vagaro** | from £30 (1 calendar) | None | ~£120/mo typical mid-size team |
| **Phorest** | £100–400 | None | Mid-to-large salons, strong marketing tools |

**The gap in the market.** Nobody in this list combines a prepaid stored-value
wallet, per-customer rebook prediction and add-on affinity scoring. The
commission players are cheap upfront and expensive at scale; the subscription
players are booking calendars with reporting bolted on.

**Our position:** priced between Vagaro's entry tier and Phorest's premium, with
**zero commission** as the headline. On a £120k shop that single fact is worth
more than the entire subscription.

## 3. The 30-day free trial

Use this the moment a client says "I'll think about it". It is the answer to that
sentence.

**The offer:**

> 30 days, completely free. No card, no contract. We install it, load your real
> services and prices, and import your customer list, so you are testing it on
> your own shop rather than a demo. On day 30 I will show you exactly what it
> earned. If it has not paid for itself, we remove it and you owe nothing.

**Terms to state plainly:**

1. Full Professional features for 30 days from install.
2. No card details taken. Nothing can be charged by accident.
3. We do the setup — install, catalogue, staff, customer import, one hour of
   training.
4. The shop's data is theirs throughout. If they walk away, they get a full SQL
   and CSV export and we delete our copy.
5. On day 30: continue (setup fee invoiced, subscription starts) or stop.
6. No auto-conversion. Billing only begins when they say yes in writing.

**Why no auto-conversion:** it is tempting, and it poisons the relationship. A
barber who discovers a surprise charge tells every other barber in town.

**What to measure during the trial** — these become your day-30 close:

| Metric | Where to find it | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wallet prepay collected | Dashboard → Prepaid all-time | Cash in the bank early |
| Add-on attach rate | Dashboard → Add-on attach rate | Up 8–15 points |
| Reminder-driven bookings | Dashboard → Where bookings came from | 20%+ of bookings |
| Referrals converted | Referral screen | 3+ new customers |
| Overdue customers recovered | Win-back list | Named, countable |

Screenshot the dashboard on day 1. Put it side by side with day 30. That
comparison closes the deal, not your pitch.

## 4. Unit economics (our side)

**Cost to serve one Professional client, per month — modelled:**

| Item | Monthly |
| --- | --- |
| Hosting (small VPS, one shop) | £5 |
| SMS (1,000 bundled at ~2.5p, ~60% used) | £15 |
| Support (modelled 30 min/mo at £30/hr) | £15 |
| Payment processing on our own fee (1.5% + 20p) | £2 |
| **Total** | **£37** |

**Gross margin: £82/month per client, or 69%.**

That is healthy for a business with a services component. It supports roughly
£1,000/month of blended acquisition and onboarding cost per client at a 12-month
payback.

**Modelled trajectory:**

| Clients | MRR | Annual recurring | Gross profit/yr |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | £1,190 | £14,280 | £9,840 |
| 25 | £2,975 | £35,700 | £24,600 |
| 50 | £5,950 | £71,400 | £49,200 |
| 100 | £11,900 | £142,800 | £98,400 |

Setup fees are on top: at 50 clients that is roughly £75k of one-off revenue,
which is what funds the sales effort.

**The number to watch is churn, not growth.** At £119/mo with a £1,499 setup
fee, a client who leaves in month six is roughly break-even. One who stays three
years is worth about £5,800. Everything in the retention features — reminders,
wallet, referrals — applies to our own business as much as theirs: the wallet
balance is why *their* customers do not leave, and the dashboard is why *they*
do not leave us.

## 5. Client-side ROI (modelled)

For a five-chair shop turning over ~£120,000/year. The `#/pricing` screen has
this as a live calculator you can run with their real figures.

**Assumptions — state these out loud:**

- 5 chairs, 8 cuts per chair per day, 6 days
- Average ticket £27
- Add-on attach improves from ~30% to ~52%
- Rebook rate improves from ~45% to ~60%, of which 35% converts to a real visit
- 35% of revenue moves to prepaid wallet at ~11% average bonus

| Line | Year 1 |
| --- | --- |
| Extra add-on revenue | +£5,600 |
| Extra visits from reminders | +£3,900 |
| Commission no longer paid (vs a 4.5% platform) | +£5,400 |
| Cost of wallet bonuses | −£4,600 |
| BarberIQ (setup £1,499 + £1,428) | −£2,927 |
| **Net year one** | **≈ +£7,400** |
| **Pays for itself in** | **≈ 3 months** |

Year two is better: no setup fee, so roughly **+£8,900**.

**The line that does the work in a meeting:** the commission saving alone
(£5,400) is nearly double the total cost of the software (£2,927). Everything
else is upside.

**Be honest about the bonus cost.** A client who spots that you hid the £4,600
stops trusting the rest of the sheet. Show it, then explain what it buys: cash
upfront, lock-in, and higher visit frequency — with full price still charged per
cut, unlike a discount.

## 6. Objection handling

**"It's too expensive."**
Compared to what? Fresha is free and takes 20% of every new marketplace client —
on your numbers that is £6,000 a year. We are £119 a month and take nothing.
Let's put your actual takings into the calculator.

**"I already use Booksy."**
Keep it for the trial month. Take bookings where you do now, and use us for the
wallet, reminders and add-ons — the three things Booksy does not do. Compare them
on day 30 and decide with evidence rather than a sales pitch.

**"I'm not technical."**
You do not touch the setup. We install it, load your prices and staff, import
your customers, and spend an hour with your team. Your barbers learn it in an
afternoon because it looks like a phone app.

**"Giving away £5 on every £50 sounds mad."**
You are buying three things: the cash today instead of in six weeks, a customer
committed to coming back to you, and a higher visit frequency. And unlike a 10%
discount you still charge full price for the cut. The bonus only costs you when
they spend — and when they spend, they are in your chair.

**"What if my customers won't use an app?"**
Most will not, at first. The wallet is the hook — it is worth real money to them.
Start by offering it to your twenty most regular customers at the chair. Once
they are prepaid, they stop going anywhere else.

**"Is my data safe? Where does the AI send it?"**
Nowhere. The intelligence runs on your own database on your own machine. No
customer record is sent to any AI service. You can export everything as SQL any
time — I will show you now if you like.

**"I want to think about it."**
Take the 30 days. Free, no card, no contract, and set up with your real data. The
worst case is you learn what your add-on attach rate actually is.

## 7. Who to sell to first

Ranked by how quickly they close:

1. **3–6 chair independents doing £100k+** — the sweet spot. Enough volume for
   the maths to be obvious, small enough that the owner decides alone.
2. **Shops already paying commission** to Fresha or Treatwell — the saving does
   your selling for you. Ask "what did the marketplace take last month?"
3. **Two-site owners** — they feel the reporting pain most and go straight to
   Multi-Site.
4. **New shops opening** — no incumbent system to displace, but no history for
   the intelligence to learn from and no cash. Lower priority.

**Avoid at first:** single-chair barbers (cannot justify £59 + setup) and large
chains (long procurement, they will want features you have not built).

**Best channel by a distance: referral between shop owners.** Barbers talk to
barbers. Build a client referral scheme with the same shape as the in-app one —
one month free for both sides — and use it. The mechanic works; you built it.
